# Game feel research: camera, jumping, juice and collectibles

This note lists concrete practices for tuning the game kit (320×180 canvas, 12 px tiles, 60 fixed steps a second), whose owner reports that the camera feels odd and that jumping and coin pickups are not satisfying. Compiled 2026-11-02. Celeste values come from its published [`Player.cs`](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs) and are in pixels per second at the same 320×180 resolution, but with 8 px tiles. Divide them by 60 for pixels per step, and multiply by 1.5 to keep the same feel relative to our 12 px tiles. Celeste Classic ([`Classic.cs`](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/PICO-8/Classic.cs), in the same repository) runs at 30 frames a second. Wikis, disassemblies and decompilations are marked "secondary", and numbers I worked out myself are marked "derived".

## 1. Cameras in side-scrollers

- **Use a camera window, not a locked camera.** The camera stays still until the player pushes an edge of the window. Rastan Saga's window is "as high as a standard jump", so ordinary jumps never move the camera. [Keren, "Scroll Back"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/scroll-back-the-theory-and-practice-of-cameras-in-side-scrollers), [GDC 2015](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022243/Scroll-Back-The-Theory-and)
- **Snap vertically when the player lands (platform snapping).** Super Mario World holds the camera still during a jump, and when Mario lands "the camera would immediately snap to its position". Rayman's window is "just over the height of Rayman's jump". [Keren](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/scroll-back-the-theory-and-practice-of-cameras-in-side-scrollers). The camera never moves *up* mid-jump, but it does follow a fall. The reason is that most jumps are made to clear something, not to climb. [nesdev, secondary](https://forums.nesdev.org/viewtopic.php?t=24689)
- **Use dual forward focus that switches only after the player commits.** Super Mario World keeps its look-ahead while Mario turns back, "until Mario hits a predefined threshold". [Keren](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/scroll-back-the-theory-and-practice-of-cameras-in-side-scrollers). On its 256 px screen, entering an outer third (x>171 or x<85) moves the rear threshold to the opposite third and the front threshold to 16 px from centre. Each frame, the camera moves to keep the player between the two. [nesdev, secondary](https://forums.nesdev.org/viewtopic.php?t=24689). At 320 px wide that gives thirds at about 107 and 213 and front thresholds at 144 and 176 (derived).
- **Move the look-ahead gradually.** In Bonanza Bros. the camera switches at about twice the player's speed, and only while the player moves. Cave Story uses a "base snapping speed plus walking speed", and Defender looks about 25% of the screen ahead. Super Mario Bros. starts speeding the camera up about 25% off-centre, so it is already at Mario's speed when he reaches the edge. [Keren](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/scroll-back-the-theory-and-practice-of-cameras-in-side-scrollers)
- **Sonic's numbers (320×224).** The horizontal border runs from x=144 to x=160, and the air band is ±32 px around y=96. On the ground, vertical catch-up is capped at 6 px per frame at low speed and 16 when fast. Sonic CD shifts its focus 64 px at 2 px per step, and only once the player is running fast. [Sonic Physics Guide, secondary (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://info.sonicretro.org/SPG:Camera)
- **Be cautious with vertical look-ahead.** Projected focus "may not work well in platforms, especially vertically, where jumps and especially landing don't extrapolate well". Reaching the bottom of Sonic's window at full falling speed is jarring. [Keren](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/scroll-back-the-theory-and-practice-of-cameras-in-side-scrollers). No source gives numbers for looking ahead during a fall. My suggestion: bias the view downwards only once a fall outlasts a normal jump arc, and move at a capped speed.
- **Make smoothing independent of frame rate, and clamp first.** Celeste clamps its target to `[Left, Right − 320] × [Top, Bottom − 180]`, then applies `cam += (target − cam) * (1 − 0.01^dt)`. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs). At 60 Hz that closes 7.4% of the gap per step, a half-life of about 9 steps (derived). The general form is `lerp(a, b, 1 − r^dt)`. [Driscoll](https://www.rorydriscoll.com/2016/03/07/frame-rate-independent-damping-using-lerp/). Eiserloh's per-frame weights at 60 fps range from 0.01 ("nice and slow") to 0.5 ("incredibly fast"), and he suggests separate weights per axis and for up versus down. [Eiserloh slides](http://www.mathforgameprogrammers.com/gdc2016/GDC2016_Eiserloh_Squirrel_JuicingYourCameras.pdf). Celeste's target is simply the player's centre, with no window. That suits small rooms, but scrolling levels need a window as well.
- **Avoid pixel-art jitter.** Actors keep integer positions plus a fractional remainder. [Thorson, "Celeste & TowerFall Physics"](https://www.maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_towerfall_physics/). Snapping a smoothed camera to the grid produces positions that "feel a bit random, hence the jittering". [Moonflower, secondary](https://apples.moonflowergames.com/articles/pixel-art-smooth-camera-anti-aliasing/). Hyper Light Drifter renders on a canvas aligned to game pixels and shifts that canvas by the sub-pixel remainder. [Keren](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/scroll-back-the-theory-and-practice-of-cameras-in-side-scrollers). To do this, use a buffer with a 1 px margin, draw the world at the rounded camera position, and offset the upscaled image by fraction × scale. Keep the HUD in a layer that is not shifted, and scale by whole numbers only to avoid shimmer. [Moonflower, secondary](https://apples.moonflowergames.com/articles/pixel-art-smooth-camera-anti-aliasing/)
- **Interpolate between fixed steps.** Render at `previous + (current − previous) * accumulator/dt`. [Fiedler](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/). Browsers run `requestAnimationFrame` at the display's rate, so 120 Hz and 144 Hz screens stutter without interpolation.
- **Use trauma-based shake.** Keep trauma between 0 and 1. Add 0.2 or 0.5 per event, decay it linearly, and shake by trauma² or trauma³ (with the cube, trauma of 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9 gives 3%, 22% and 73% shake). Use Perlin noise rather than random values, so shake also behaves under pause and slow motion. Add the shake on top of the base camera, and in 2D use translation plus rotation. [Eiserloh slides](http://www.mathforgameprogrammers.com/gdc2016/GDC2016_Eiserloh_Squirrel_JuicingYourCameras.pdf), [talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tu-Qe66AvtY). For scale, Celeste Classic's dash shake is about ±2 px for 6 frames. [Classic.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/PICO-8/Classic.cs). Celeste uses directional shakes of 0.1–0.2 s along dashes and bounces. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)
- **Respect reduced motion.** "Avoid the use of camera shake … or provide an option to turn off these behaviors". The guideline cites Halo Infinite's 0–100% sliders. [XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117). Vlambeer added an option to disable screen shake in Nuclear Throne "because some people were getting really nauseous". [Nijman](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U). Use `prefers-reduced-motion` for the defaults. [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)

## 2. Jump and movement feel

- **Design the jump by apex height and time to apex.** `v0 = 2h/t_h` and `g = 2h/t_h²`. [Pittman slides](http://www.mathforgameprogrammers.com/gdc2016/GDC2016_Pittman_Kyle_BuildingABetterJump.pdf), [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023148/Math-for-Game-Programmers-Building), [talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hG9SzQxaCm8). For example, a 40 px apex reached in 21 steps gives `v0 = 3.81 px/step` and `g = 0.181 px/step²` (derived). Integrate with `pos += v·dt + ½a·dt²`, which is exact while acceleration is constant. [Pittman](http://www.mathforgameprogrammers.com/gdc2016/GDC2016_Pittman_Kyle_BuildingABetterJump.pdf)
- **Fall faster than you rise.** Pittman chains parabolic arcs, switching gravity at each break while keeping position and velocity continuous. Super Mario Bros.' falling gravity is "about three times normal gravity". Swink's other jump parameters are minimum and maximum hold time and terminal velocity. [Swink, *Game Feel*](https://www.routledge.com/Game-Feel-A-Game-Designers-Guide-to-Virtual-Sensation/Swink/p/book/9780123743282)
- **Make jump height variable.** Celeste has gravity 900 px/s² and a jump speed of −105 px/s. It holds that speed for up to 0.2 s (12 steps) while jump is held. Releasing ends the hold, and so does hitting a ceiling more than 0.05 s in. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs). Simulated, a full jump peaks at about 27 px after 20 steps, and a tap peaks at about 5 px (derived).
- **Add apex hang.** Celeste applies half gravity while jump is held and `|vy| < 40 px/s`. Thorson: "it's subtle, but this gives you more time to adjust for landing". [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs), [Thorson's thread](https://twitter.com/MaddyThorson/status/1238338574220546049), [Celeste & Forgiveness](https://www.maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/)
- **Give coyote time and buffer jumps.** Celeste's coyote time is 0.1 s (6 steps). [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs). Celeste Classic's coyote time is 6 frames (0.2 s) and its buffer 4 frames (0.13 s). [Classic.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/PICO-8/Classic.cs). Celeste's buffer is 0.08 s. [decompiled Input.cs, secondary](https://github.com/TheCyndaquilDecompilers/Celeste_Decompiled/blob/main/Celeste/Input.cs). A buffered press should "jump on the exact frame that you land". [Celeste & Forgiveness](https://www.maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/)
- **Correct corners and assist one-way platforms.** When the player rises into a ceiling corner, Celeste tries 1–4 px sideways (and 1 px up) in the direction of travel. Dashes into a ledge corner get the same 4 px. A player rising through a one-way platform is nudged up at 40 px/s. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs). For our tiles, about 6 px of correction is the equivalent (derived). Wall jumps work from 2 px away, because "everything is fudged a tiny bit in the player's favor". [Celeste & Forgiveness](https://www.maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/)
- **Accelerate and turn quickly.** Celeste uses `MaxRun = 90`, `RunAccel = 1000` and `AirMult = 0.65`, and a jump adds 40 px/s in the held direction. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs). Reaching full speed or stopping takes about 5–6 steps, and a full reversal about 11 (derived).
- **Cap the fall speed and tune water.** Celeste's fall cap is 160 px/s (2.67 px per step), rising to 240 when the player holds down. Entering water halves a falling player's speed, and swimming tops out at 80 px/s with an acceleration of 600. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)
- **Respond within the same step.** "At 50ms, response feels instantaneous. Above 100ms, the lag is noticeable but ignorable. At 200ms, the response feels sluggish." [Swink](https://www.routledge.com/Game-Feel-A-Game-Designers-Guide-to-Virtual-Sensation/Swink/p/book/9780123743282)

## 3. Juice and game feel

- **Polish sells the physics without changing it.** Swink defines polish as "any effect that artificially enhances interaction without changing the underlying simulation". He adds that "constraints define sensation", so tune gap and ledge spacing against the movement. [Swink](https://www.routledge.com/Game-Feel-A-Game-Designers-Guide-to-Virtual-Sensation/Swink/p/book/9780123743282)
- **Nijman's tricks that suit a platformer.** They are animation and sound, impact effects, a white hit flash, enemy knockback ("move them back a couple pixels"), permanence (shells "stay forever"), camera lerp, screen shake, weapon kick, more bass, and sleep. On sleep: "when I hit an enemy it will pause for 20 milliseconds or something". He warns that shake is addictive and that you stop noticing how much you have added. [Nijman, "The Art of Screenshake"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U)
- **Keep hit-stop short.** Celeste freezes for 0.05 s (3 steps) on a dash and 0.1 s on big launches. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs). Juicy Breakout exposes a freeze of 0–320 ms with a time-scale multiplier and fade-in and fade-out ramps of 0–160 ms. [Settings.as](https://github.com/grapefrukt/juicy-breakout/blob/master/src/com/grapefrukt/games/juicy/Settings.as)
- **Tween, stretch, flash and add sound.** Juice is "maximum output for minimum input". The talk shows eased tweens with random delays, stretching by velocity, a scale-up on impact that eases back, a white flash on hits, particles and a trail. It calls shake "powerful" and sound "the most cost effective thing you can add". Each effect has its own toggle. [Jonasson & Purho](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg), [source](https://github.com/grapefrukt/juicy-breakout)

## 4. Collectibles (coins, stars)

- **Raise the pitch on chained pickups.** PopCap's Kapalka: "always have sound cues of rising pitch associated with combos". [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/2008/02/peggle-creator/). Peggle 2's peg hits "play ascending diatonic scales" in harmony with the music. [G.A.N.G.](https://www.audiogang.org/peggle2-sonic-joy/). Peggle Blast resets to the first note on each shot and advances one note per hit, building scales from semitone steps (major is 2 2 1 2 2 2 1). [Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/blog/peggle-blast-peg-hits-and-the-music-system/). Celeste chains strawberries with an index and a reset timer whose length is not published. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)
- **Alternate stereo sides and mark milestones.** Sonic 1 toggles `v_ring_speaker` on every ring, so pickups alternate left and right, and every 100 rings awards a life with a jingle. [s1disasm, secondary](https://github.com/sonicretro/s1disasm/blob/c1ed7c78f28bb7abee97bf5f3bec66f6f8e4d9c4/Variables.asm)
- **Pop the pickup.** Scale it up and ease it back, flash white and burst particles. [Juice it or lose it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg). Celeste's bursts use 4–8 particles at this resolution. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)
- **No primary numbers for the rest.** I found none for a magnet radius, flying the pickup to the HUD, a HUD counter bump or score pop-ups. They are common practice, so tune them by feel.

## 5. Jumping and landing

- **Stretch on takeoff.** Celeste sets the sprite scale to (0.6, 1.4), bursts 4 dust particles upward from the feet and plays a sound specific to the jump type. Super bounces use (0.5, 1.5). [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)
- **Squash on landing in proportion to impact.** Celeste uses `t = min(vy/240, 1)`, `scaleX = lerp(1, 1.6, t)` and `scaleY = lerp(1, 0.4, t)`, which gives (1.4, 0.6) at normal maximum fall speed. Landings at `vy ≥ 80` also burst 8 dust particles. Every landing plays a land sound chosen by surface, plus a short light rumble. [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)
- **Recover quickly.** Scale returns towards 1 at 1.75 per second, about 14 steps from a 0.4 deviation (derived). Scale around the feet so the sprite stays on the floor (my note). [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)
- **Vary the pitch.** One tutorial uses a random window of ±1 semitone for repeated sounds. [PlayerMotion, secondary](https://playermotion.games/lessons/fmod-game-audio/randomize-footsteps). No primary source gives a range.

## 6. Other platformer practices

- **Invulnerability after a hit.** Shovel Knight "flashes on and off during his 'invincible' state". Its damage flash is "consistent across all objects". [Yacht Club, "Breaking the NES"](https://www.yachtclubgames.com/blog/breaking-the-nes/). In Hollow Knight, invulnerability lasts 1.3 s and the knockback takes away input for 0.2 s. [Hollow Knight Wiki, secondary](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Stalwart_Shell)
- **Readability.** Yacht Club limited particles, used parallax to make "the gameplay layer more readable", and simplified sprites. [Breaking the NES](https://www.yachtclubgames.com/blog/breaking-the-nes/)
- **Fast respawn.** Difficulty is the chance of dying × the penalty for dying, so Super Meat Boy's respawn is "almost instantaneous" and its levels are short. [McMillen](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/-i-super-meat-boy-i-s-mcmillen-explains-why-so-hard-)
- **Checkpoints.** Shovel Knight's checkpoints trigger when walked past, show a visible flame, and need three hits to break. [Yacht Club](https://www.yachtclubgames.com/blog/check-point-design/). Eight per level "were just too many". [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2014/07/29/shovel-knight-devs-discuss-co-op-pogo-jumping-and-beyond)
- **Onboarding through level design.** World 1-1 lets players "gradually and naturally understand what they're doing". [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-miyamoto-built-i-super-mario-bros-i-legendary-world-1-1). The Goomba exists because a Koopa was "too difficult for the player to jump on and then kick". [Eurogamer video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRGRJRUWafY), quoted by [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/originals/362525-perfect-level-super-mario-bros-world-1-1-changed-everything)
- **Telegraph enemy attacks.** Give each attack a visible wind-up before its hitbox becomes active. I found no primary source for durations.
- **Inputs.** When left and right are held together, Celeste lets the newer press win (`TakeNewer`). [decompiled Input.cs, secondary](https://github.com/TheCyndaquilDecompilers/Celeste_Decompiled/blob/main/Celeste/Input.cs)
- **Apply reduced motion to particles.** XAG 117 also covers non-interactive movement. [XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117). Cut decorative particles, but keep flashes that carry gameplay information (my recommendation).

## Checklist for a small pixel-art platformer

- [ ] The camera has a horizontal window with dual forward focus, and it switches side only after the player enters the outer third.
- [ ] The look-ahead moves at a speed tied to the player's speed, never in one jump.
- [ ] The camera never moves up mid-jump. It re-targets when the player lands at a new height, and it follows falls.
- [ ] Vertical catch-up after a landing is smoothed or capped (Sonic: 6 px per frame), and downward look-ahead starts only after a long fall.
- [ ] Smoothing uses `1 − r^dt` (Celeste: `r = 0.01`), applied after clamping the target to level bounds.
- [ ] Actors store integer positions plus a remainder.
- [ ] The world is drawn at one rounded camera position, and sub-pixel camera movement shifts the upscaled buffer, which has a 1 px margin.
- [ ] The HUD is drawn in its own layer, which is not shifted, and scaling uses whole numbers only.
- [ ] Rendering interpolates between fixed steps on displays faster than 60 Hz.
- [ ] Shake uses trauma²: add 0.2–0.5 per event, decay linearly, and use smooth noise.
- [ ] Shake is at most about 2 px, with little or no rotation.
- [ ] Hits and stomps use short directional kicks.
- [ ] `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off shake and decorative particles, and there is a 0–100% shake slider.
- [ ] Jump values are derived from apex height and steps: `v0 = 2h/n`, `g = 2h/n²`.
- [ ] Falling gravity is heavier than rising gravity (Super Mario Bros.: about 3×).
- [ ] Releasing jump early cuts the jump short, and there is a minimum height.
- [ ] Apex hang: half gravity while jump is held and |vy| is below about 0.67 px per step.
- [ ] Coyote time is 6 steps and the buffer 5–7 steps, and a buffered jump fires on the landing step.
- [ ] Corner correction is up to about 6 px, and rising through one-way platforms gets an assist.
- [ ] Full speed is reached in about 6 steps, a reversal takes about 11, and air control is 0.65×.
- [ ] Fall speed is capped, and entering water halves the falling speed.
- [ ] Takeoff stretches to (0.6, 1.4) at the feet, with 4 dust particles and a jump sound.
- [ ] Landing squashes up to (1.4, 0.6) depending on fall speed, with 8 dust particles on hard landings and a landing sound.
- [ ] Scale recovers in about 14 steps.
- [ ] Repeated sounds get a random pitch offset of ±1 semitone.
- [ ] Coin chains step up a major scale and reset after a timeout.
- [ ] Coin sounds alternate stereo pan.
- [ ] Pickups pop, flash and burst 4–8 particles, and the HUD counter bumps.
- [ ] Hits get 1–3 steps of hit-stop.
- [ ] Enemies flash white and are knocked back a few pixels.
- [ ] After a hit, the hero flashes and is invulnerable for about a second.
- [ ] Enemy attacks have a visible wind-up.
- [ ] Respawn is near-instant, and the checkpoint lights up visibly on contact.
- [ ] The first level teaches by placement, not text.
- [ ] Every juice effect has its own toggle.
